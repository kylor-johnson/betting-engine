## Rules for any pick (from my feedback)
1. **Price comes first.** If the value was in the opener and the line has already moved, it's gone. Only bet where today's number still beats a fair line.
2. **Every bet needs a reason besides line movement.** That means an injury or matchup edge the line may not fully reflect. "Sharps already moved it" is not enough on its own.
3. **Give a "don't bet past" number for every pick.** If my book is worse, I pass.
4. **Watch key numbers.** In football, 3 and 7 are the most common final margins. Losing the half point past +3 or +7 can wipe out the value.
5. **Size bets small.** Max 1 unit ($25) today. Conditional bets are 0.5u. Walters' rule: never more than 3% of bankroll on one game.
6. **Size down bets that rely on the same thing.** Two bets that both depend on the same kind of injury can lose together.
7. **Injury-dependent bets wait for the final availability report**, about 90 minutes to 2 hours before kickoff.
8. **Check every number twice and confirm the current line before recommending.** Earlier mistakes in this chat: a first card built mostly from sharp-money signals without re-pricing, and a math error ($275 vs. $300).

## Methods we're using
- **Billy Walters** (book "Gambler", via Covers' summary): power ratings produce a predicted score you compare to the line. Price injuries: a QB is about a touchdown, top non-QBs 2.5-3 points, most players about zero. Shop for the best number, especially around key numbers. Bet favorites early and dogs late. Bet 0.5-3 units, max 3% of bankroll per event. Never chase losses.
- **Collin Wilson** (Action Network): treat a bet like a stock and buy low against the market. His power ratings use advanced stats, coaching changes and returning-player continuity. His "Havoc" stat (sacks, tackles for loss, INTs, pass breakups) flags offenses likely to make mistakes. Note: Action Network's own ratings make Ohio State -3.5 at Nebraska, which is exactly the market.
- **Market signals**: reverse line movement (the line moves against the side most tickets are on), a big gap between a side's share of money and its share of tickets, and beating the closing line (closing line value).
