# 12a. What the first Wagerhouse-versus-Pinnacle measurement says (Nov 23, 2026)

Numbers are in `12-wagerhouse-vs-pinnacle.md` (regenerate with `engine wh-edge`). The sample is two days of hourly reads: 25 NFL games, 16 NBA, 79 college; only 4 to 12 games per small market have kicked off. Read this as a first look, not a finding.

1. **The small markets are not soft; they are more expensive.** Wagerhouse's juice is 4.7 to 4.8% on full-game sides and totals against Pinnacle's 2.6 to 3.2%. On first quarters and team totals it is 6.3 to 7.0% against 3.4 to 4.3%. Average gap between the two books' numbers is only 0.2 to 0.35 points everywhere. With Pinnacle as the truth, the better side is worth -1.5% to -4.7% on average in every market, worst on team totals and first quarters.
2. **Price clears the engine's 2% bar rarely** (0 to 9% of reads per market) and the closing-line value on those reads is a handful of reads from 1 to 3 games, which says nothing yet.
3. **Speed is the only place with a hint.** Of Pinnacle's half-point moves, Wagerhouse showed the same move in the same hourly read most of the time, but on NFL sides it took 1 to 3 hours twice (median 2.6h) and on NFL totals about 6 hours twice (median 5.8h). That is 5 and 10 moves; the hourly resolution cannot say how long inside the hour.
4. **What it changes.** Do not put research time into first quarters, halves or team totals at Wagerhouse on price grounds. Keep logging them (cheap). The edge question stays where the design already put it: the minutes after news, on full-game sides and totals.
5. **What would answer it properly:** reads every 10 to 15 minutes for a few windows (Wednesday to Friday practice reports, Sunday inactives about 90 minutes before kickoff), to see how many minutes Wagerhouse trails Pinnacle after a move. That changes the read schedule and needs Kylor's go.
